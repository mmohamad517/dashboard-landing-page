Children remember what they argued about and what they got wrong. They forget what they were told. That is the whole principle behind teaching outdoor skills as a game rather than a briefing.

## Beat the clock

Pick a physical task — pitch a tarp shelter, filter cloudy water through cloth, tie a taut-line hitch. Two teams, same task, same start. First working result wins.

Works because there is a visible right answer: the shelter stands or it does not.

## Priority sort

Lay out five situations or items. As a group, rank them by what you would deal with first. Then change the setting — desert, forest, snow — and watch the order change.

This is the one that produces the best arguments, and it teaches the most, because the ranking genuinely does change with conditions.

## Do it blind

Say only what the task is — "find north" — and let them try before anyone explains the method. Then show them. The gap between their attempt and the method is the part they remember.

## Hard choices

Pose a scenario with no comfortable answer. *You have two days of water and three days of walking.* Everyone commits to an answer out loud before anyone hears the reasoning.

The commitment matters. Once you have defended a position, you remember why it was wrong.

## Teach-back

Each person learns one skill and teaches it to everyone else. Explaining it exposes the parts they had not really understood, and children take the responsibility seriously.

## Keeping it safe

- Anything involving flame or a knife needs an adult running it, not supervising from a distance.
- Practise at home first — garden, park, kitchen table.
- Never make wild food part of a game. Not plants, not mushrooms, not "which of these is edible". [There is no safe field test](/articles/how-long-can-you-survive-without-food/), and framing it as play is how a game becomes a hospital visit.
- Emergency scenarios should end with "and then we call for help", so that stays the reflex.

## Why this works better than a briefing

Nobody retains a lecture delivered in a car park. Everybody retains the evening they were confidently wrong in front of their family — and that is a much better place to be wrong than on a trail.
