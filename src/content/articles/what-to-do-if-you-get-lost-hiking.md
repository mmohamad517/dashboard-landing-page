The moment you realise you are not sure where you are, stop walking. The urge to press on and recognise something is what turns briefly unsure into genuinely lost.

## Sit down

Literally. Take the pack off, drink something, eat something. Ten minutes of this does more for your decision-making than an hour of walking, because low blood sugar and mild dehydration degrade judgement before you notice.

## Then think

- When were you last certain of your position?
- How long ago, and roughly what direction have you walked since?
- Can you see any landmark you recognise — a ridge line, a river, a road?
- Does anyone know your route and when you are due back?

That last question decides the next one.

## Stay put, or move?

**Stay put if** someone knows your route and expects you back, you have shelter and water, or the light is going. This is the right answer far more often than people expect. Searches that start from a known route are short.

**Consider moving if** nobody knows where you are, or you can actually see your destination — not guess at it, see it.

If you do move, pick one direction and hold it. Following water downstream tends toward people, at the cost of energy and daylight. Mark your route as you go so you can retrace it.

## Make yourself findable

Bright kit spread out, geometric shapes, [signalling in threes](/articles/how-to-signal-for-help-in-the-wilderness/). Get big rather than loud.

## Get warm before you get worried

Exposure works faster than thirst and much faster than hunger. Out of the wind, off the ground, dry layers on. [Shelter](/articles/how-to-build-an-emergency-shelter/) comes before almost everything else on the list.

## What not to do

Do not walk after dark in rough ground. Do not split the group. Do not push on to "just have a look" past the point where turning back is easy. And do not start foraging — [food is the last priority](/articles/how-long-can-you-survive-without-food/), and the risk is real.
