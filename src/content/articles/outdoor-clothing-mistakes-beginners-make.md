Most beginner outdoor clothing problems come down to five repeatable mistakes — not bad luck with weather, but decisions made before the trip even starts, all of which have simple fixes.

## Mistake 1: Wearing cotton

Cotton feels comfortable at the trailhead and stays wet (and cold) once you sweat or hit rain. **Fix:** swap in a merino wool or synthetic wicking base layer — the single highest-impact change for comfort.

## Mistake 2: Overdressing at the trailhead

Feeling comfortable standing still at the start almost guarantees overheating within the first climb. **Fix:** start slightly cold on purpose; you'll warm up within ten minutes of moving.

## Mistake 3: Wearing the mid layer all day

Sweating through insulation makes it wet, and wet insulation stops working. **Fix:** treat the mid layer as something you add at rest stops and remove while moving, not a fixed part of your outfit.

## Mistake 4: Ignoring hands, feet, and head

Extremities lose heat disproportionately fast, and a great jacket doesn't fix cold hands or wet feet. **Fix:** budget real attention (and dry backups) for gloves, socks, and a hat — not just your main jacket.

## Mistake 5: Packing a shell you can't reach quickly

A rain shell buried at the bottom of your bag doesn't get used until you're already soaked. **Fix:** keep your shell in an easily accessible pocket or the top of your pack, every time.

## Why these mistakes are so common

Most of this advice contradicts how people dress for everyday life, where "put on a warm coat and you're set" is genuinely fine. Trail conditions change fast enough, and effort levels swing widely enough, that the same approach fails outdoors — not because beginners are doing something unusually wrong, but because normal clothing habits don't transfer.

## The fastest way to fix all five at once

Adopt the three-layer system (base, mid, shell) as a default rather than relearning each mistake individually — nearly all five mistakes above are really one mistake (treating clothing as a single fixed outfit) showing up in different ways.
