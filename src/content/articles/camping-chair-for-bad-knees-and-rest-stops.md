Most camping chair advice ranks by weight and price and stops there. That works fine if getting up and down from a low seat is effortless for you. It isn't for everyone — older campers, anyone with knee or hip pain, people recovering from an injury, or a parent who just needs a real place to sit while young kids take a break — and almost no gear guide mentions the one spec that actually decides whether a chair works for that: seat height.

## Why seat height is the spec that actually matters

Standing up from a low seat takes more knee bend and more leg strength than standing up from a higher one — that's just how the mechanics of getting up work, regardless of how padded or lightweight the chair is. A classic low-slung beach or festival chair, the kind that sits close to the ground to save weight and pack size, can turn every single sit-down into a small effort. Over a full day at camp, that adds up in a way a spec sheet never shows.

None of this shows up in a typical "best camping chairs" roundup, because those are written around weight and packed size, not around who's actually sitting in the chair.

## What to actually check before buying

- **Seat height off the ground.** Higher is generally easier to rise from. A chair in the high-30cm-to-40cm range sits closer to a normal dining chair than a beach chair does.
- **Armrests.** A real place to push up from with your hands takes real load off your knees — this matters as much as the seat height itself.
- **A stable, braced base.** A chair that wobbles or flexes under weight makes standing up harder and less confident, regardless of seat height.
- **Backrest height and support**, if the person will be sitting for a while rather than just a quick pause.
- **Weight**, last. A chair that's genuinely easier to get in and out of is worth carrying a bit more of, especially for car camping where it isn't being carried far.

## Quick answers

**Why does camping chair seat height matter?**
A higher seat requires less knee bend and less leg strength to stand up from, which matters for older campers, anyone with knee or hip pain, or after a long day when getting up from a low chair gets harder.

**What seat height should I look for in a camping chair for bad knees?**
Something closer to a normal dining chair than a beach chair — roughly high-30cm to 40cm off the ground rather than the low, close-to-the-ground design common on ultralight beach chairs.

**Do armrests actually matter for a camping chair?**
Yes — a real armrest to push up from takes load off the knees when standing, which matters as much as seat height for anyone who finds getting up from a low chair difficult.

**Is a heavier camping chair worth it for comfort?**
For car camping specifically, often yes — a slightly heavier, taller, more stable chair that's actually easy to get in and out of is worth more than shaving off a kilogram you won't notice while it's in the car.
