A weekend camping trip — typically two nights — needs enough clothing and supplies for two full days without tipping into the over-preparation of a week-long packing list, which is the most common mistake first-time weekend campers make.

## Clothing for two nights

One hiking or activity outfit per day (two total, since you can re-wear layers rather than packing a fresh set for every hour), one dedicated warm outfit for evenings around camp, and a dry sleep set that never doubles as daytime clothing. Two pairs of extra socks covers unexpected wet feet without overpacking.

## Sleep and shelter

A tent sized correctly for your group (not maximally large "for comfort," which just adds weight and setup time), a sleeping bag rated for the coldest expected night, and a sleeping pad. For a weekend trip, one pillow per person is a reasonable comfort item that doesn't cost much space.

## Food for two days

Plan meals specifically rather than overpacking "extra just in case" — for a weekend, that's roughly 4–6 planned meals plus snacks. A cooler with ice handles a weekend's worth of perishables without spoiling, which isn't always true on longer trips.

## The over-packing trap

Weekend trips are short enough that most "backup" items never get used — a second stove, three changes of daytime clothes, or enough food for five days all just take up space and make packing and loading the car take longer than it needs to. If an item doesn't have a specific use across your actual two-day, two-night plan, leave it.

## A simple checklist structure

Organize by category — clothing, sleep, cooking, safety — and fill in only what this specific trip's length and forecast require, rather than working from a generic "everything you might ever need" master list.
