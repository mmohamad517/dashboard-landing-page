Very few beginner camping problems are equipment problems. They are decisions — usually made calmly, hours before anything felt like it mattered.

## Nobody knew the plan

The single most consequential mistake. A search that starts with a known route and a known return time is short. One that starts with "he mentioned the mountains" is not.

Tell one person: where you are going, your route, when you will be back, what your vehicle looks like and where it is parked, and what they should do if you are late. Then message them when you are out.

It costs one message and it changes everything.

## The turnaround time that was ignored

Groups agree a turnaround time and then push past it, because the summit is close and the weather seems fine.

Almost every serious mountain incident includes exactly that decision. Set the time, and honour it. The summit will be there next month.

## Camping in the wrong place

- **In a dry watercourse.** Flat, sheltered, sandy — and the reason it is flat and sandy is that water moves through it. [Flash floods](/articles/flash-flood-safety-for-campers/) arrive under clear skies.
- **Under a dead branch.** Look up before you pitch. Every time.
- **At the bottom of a hollow**, where cold air pools overnight.
- **On the only flat spot on a ridge**, exposed to everything.

## Underestimating the dark

Sunset is not the deadline; usable light ends earlier than that, especially in valleys and forest. Arriving at camp with an hour of light left is comfortable. Arriving with ten minutes is how tents get pitched badly and things get lost.

Carry a head torch even on day walks you plan to finish early.

## Cotton, and being wet

Cotton holds water against your skin and pulls heat out fast. It is fine on a warm dry day and genuinely dangerous when cold and wet. This is the one clothing mistake worth caring about.

## Too much gear, badly chosen

A pack heavy enough to end the trip early has its own risk. Every "just in case" item should answer a specific question about this trip and this forecast.

## Not testing anything first

The tent nobody has pitched. The stove nobody has lit. The boots straight out of the box. First time should not be in the dark, in the rain, at the end of a long day.

Pitch it in the garden. Light it in the kitchen. Walk the boots in.

## Ignoring the early signs

Someone going quiet, fumbling with a zip, or making poor decisions is telling you something before they can say it. So is a headache in the heat.

The pattern worth memorising: **confusion means stop and act**, in the heat and in the cold alike.
