Fall camping clothing has to cover a wider daily temperature swing than any other season — a warm, sunny 65°F afternoon and a damp, 35°F night are both realistic on the same trip, which is why fall is where a proper layering system earns its keep more than in summer or the depths of winter.

## Daytime layers

A base layer and a light mid layer usually cover a fall afternoon, with a packable shell in your bag for wind or an afternoon shower — fall weather changes faster than the forecast updates, especially near mountains or large bodies of water. Save the heaviest insulation for evening rather than wearing it during active hours, when you'll just sweat through it.

## Evening and night layers

Once the sun drops, fall temperatures fall fast. Bring a genuinely warm mid layer (fleece or puffy) for sitting around camp, dry socks, a warm hat, and a sleeping bag rated colder than the average forecast temperature — the average doesn't protect you on the one cold night that dips below it.

## Managing damp conditions

Fall brings dew, morning fog, and rain more often than summer. A waterproof layer for your pack (or a pack cover) and a dry stuff sack for your sleep clothes keep your night-layer options intact even if your day layers get damp. Wet gear that never fully dries overnight is one of the most common fall-camping complaints, and it's largely preventable with separate wet and dry clothing systems.

## A simple fall packing rule

Pack for the coldest hour of the trip (usually just before dawn), not the average daytime temperature — the average will take care of itself, but the cold hour is where people get caught underdressed.
