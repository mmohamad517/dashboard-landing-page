Seasonal hiking outfits don't need to be four separate wardrobes — the same base, mid, and shell pieces work year-round; what changes between seasons is fabric weight, how many layers you're actually wearing, and how much attention your extremities need.

## Spring

Unpredictable is the defining trait: a warm afternoon and a cold, wet morning can both happen on the same trail. A lightweight base layer, a packable mid layer, and a waterproof shell cover most spring conditions — the shell earns its space more in spring than almost any other season.

## Summer

The lightest version of the system: a breathable, sun-protective base layer, a shell packed for a possible afternoon storm, and often no mid layer at all except at higher elevations or for early starts before sunrise. Sun protection (hat, buff) becomes as important as insulation in summer.

## Fall

The widest single-day temperature swing of any season. Layer light for daytime activity and pack a genuinely warm layer for evening — see the [full fall breakdown](/articles/what-to-wear-camping-in-fall/) for the specifics.

## Winter

The heaviest version of the same three layers, plus real attention to hands, feet, and head — see the [winter layering system](/articles/winter-hiking-layering-system/) for the complete approach.

## The pieces that carry across every season

A good merino or synthetic base layer, a packable mid layer, and a wind/rain shell work in all four seasons — you're adjusting weight and how many pieces you're actively wearing, not buying a new wardrobe. This is also the most budget-efficient way to build an outdoor clothing kit: three solid, versatile pieces beat a closet full of single-season items.
