There is no single correct number, and anyone quoting one is guessing at your conditions. What matters is knowing what moves the answer and planning around refills.

## What changes how much you need

- **Heat.** The single biggest factor.
- **Effort and elevation gain.** Climbing costs far more than flat distance.
- **Humidity.** Dry air steals water you never notice losing.
- **Altitude.** Increases losses through breathing.
- **Sun exposure.** Open ridges cost more than shaded forest.
- **You.** Body size and personal sweat rate vary enormously.

A hot, exposed climb can require several times what a cool, shaded, flat walk of the same distance does.

## Plan around refills, not capacity

Carrying every litre you will need is heavy and often unnecessary. The better question is: where can I refill, how far apart are those points, and what do I need to get between them with margin?

That turns water from a weight problem into a route-planning problem — and it means carrying a way to [treat water](/articles/how-to-purify-water-in-the-wild/) rather than more bottles.

In genuinely dry country with no reliable sources, this flips: you carry it all, plus a real reserve.

## Do not ration into uselessness

This one surprises people. Water in your body is doing more good than water in your bottle.

Dehydration takes your decision-making long before it takes your legs, and bad decisions are what turn a long day into an incident. Sipping steadily through the day beats saving it for an emergency that your poor judgement then creates.

Pacing is sensible. Refusing to drink is not.

## Signs you are behind

- Dark urine, or not needing to go
- Headache
- Feeling unusually tired for the effort
- Irritability, or difficulty with simple decisions

By the time you feel properly thirsty you are already behind.

## Salt

On long, hot, sweaty days, water alone is not the whole answer — cramps are often a salt problem. Electrolyte tablets weigh nothing. Salty snacks do the same job.

## Practical habits

- Drink before you start.
- Carry it where you will actually use it — a bladder hose or bottles on the shoulder strap beat a bottle at the bottom of your pack.
- Fill up at every reliable source, even when you are not low.
- In cold weather, keep it from freezing: bottles upside down, or inside your jacket.
