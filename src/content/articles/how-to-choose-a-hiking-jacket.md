Choosing a hiking jacket starts with one question: what job is it doing — blocking wind, blocking rain, or providing insulation — because a single jacket rarely does all three well, and buying based on that question prevents the common mistake of overspending on one "do everything" jacket.

## Step 1: Identify the job

If your main risk is wind and light weather, a wind shell is lighter, more breathable, and cheaper than a full rain shell. If sustained rain is likely, prioritize a genuinely waterproof-breathable shell, accepting reduced breathability as the tradeoff. If cold rather than precipitation is the main concern, you're actually shopping for a mid layer (insulation), not a shell.

## Step 2: Match it to your actual conditions

Buy for the conditions you hike in most often, not the most extreme trip you might someday take. A hiker doing mostly moderate three-season day hikes doesn't need an expedition-grade winter shell; that jacket will be overkill, overbudget, and often less comfortable in the mild conditions where it's actually used most.

## Step 3: Check fit over a layer, not alone

Try a shell or mid layer on over your actual base layer (or a similar-thickness layer), not just a t-shirt — a jacket that fits well alone can feel restrictive once you're layering underneath it on a real hike, especially across the shoulders and arms where movement matters most.

## Step 4: Prioritize function over brand

Waterproofing rating, breathability, and packability matter more for actual trail performance than brand name. A mid-range jacket that fits your real conditions well will outperform an expensive one bought for conditions you rarely encounter.

## A simple decision framework

| Your main risk | Buy this |
|---|---|
| Wind, mild weather | Lightweight wind shell |
| Sustained rain | Waterproof-breathable rain shell |
| Cold, not precipitation | Insulated mid layer, not a shell |
