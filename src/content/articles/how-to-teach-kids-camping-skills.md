Give a child one real job, and they will own it. Give them a safety briefing, and they will nod through it. The difference is whether the outcome visibly depends on them.

## One job, and it is genuinely theirs

Not a token task. Something the camp actually needs: the tent pegs, the water, checking the fire is properly out, the head torch inventory. If it does not get done, it is noticeably not done.

Rotate jobs between trips so they collect skills rather than a specialism.

## Roughly by age

**Small.** Carrying their own small pack, collecting kindling, staying within a set boundary, knowing the whistle rule.

**Middle.** Pitching a tent with help, using a whistle to signal, filtering and boiling water, laying a fire that an adult lights, reading a simple map.

**Older.** Route planning, cooking a meal for the group, navigating a leg, running the shelter build, being the one who knows the emergency plan.

## The rules that never bend

- **The whistle rule.** Everyone carries one. Three blasts means "I need you", and it is always answered without annoyance, every single time — including false alarms. The moment a child fears being told off for using it, the whistle is useless.
- **Never anything wild goes in a mouth.** Not berries, not mushrooms, not leaves. No exceptions, no clever tests, no "unless an adult says". This is the rule that should be absolutely rigid.
- **Fire has a keeper.** One named adult, present.
- **Nobody walks alone.**

## Let them be cold once, briefly, safely

A child who has been slightly cold because they left the fleece in the tent understands layering in a way no explanation achieves. Somewhere safe, briefly, with the fix right there.

The same principle covers most of it: let the small mistake happen where it is harmless.

## Teach the decision, not just the skill

Pitching a tent is a skill. Choosing where to pitch it is a decision — not in the dry riverbed, not under the dead branch, not at the bottom of the cold hollow. Decisions transfer to situations you have not trained for. Skills do not.

## Practise at home

Pitch the tent in the garden. Cook one camp meal in the kitchen. Do the seven-day skills challenge in the week before the trip. Everything is easier the second time, and the first time should not be in the dark in the rain.
