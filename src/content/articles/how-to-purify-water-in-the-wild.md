Boiling is the method that works everywhere, needs no batteries or spare parts, and handles every biological threat. If you can boil, boil.

## Boiling

Bring the water to a rolling boil. That is the point at which it is safe — the "how many minutes" arguments matter far less than actually reaching a proper boil. At high altitude, hold it a little longer.

Boiling does nothing about chemical contamination or heavy metals, which is why you still choose your source sensibly: flowing water above human activity beats a stagnant pool below a farm.

## Filtering, and what it does not do

Pouring water through a bandana, coffee filter or clean sock removes grit, leaf matter and sediment. It does not remove the things that make you ill. Cloth filtering is a preparation step, not a treatment.

A proper backpacking filter is different — a real filter with a rated pore size removes bacteria and protozoa. Most do not remove viruses, which matters more in some regions than others.

## Improvised sediment filter

If your only water is thick with silt, cut the base off a bottle, invert it, and layer cloth, then charcoal from a cold campfire, then sand, then gravel. Pour slowly.

What comes out is clearer. It is not safe. Boil it.

## Chemical treatment

Tablets and drops are light, cheap and slow. They need contact time — read the packet, and double it in cold water. They work poorly in cloudy water, which is another argument for filtering the sediment out first.

## The order that works

1. Choose the best source you can reach.
2. Let it settle, then pour off the clear part.
3. Filter out the sediment through cloth.
4. Boil, or treat.

## Finding water in the first place

Follow the landscape downhill. Look for greener, denser vegetation than the ground around it. Check the outside bend of a dry watercourse and dig at the lowest shaded point. Insect traffic and converging animal tracks are better indicators than any single plant.

Dig in shade, in the cool part of the day. Sweating out more water than you find is a real risk in dry country.
