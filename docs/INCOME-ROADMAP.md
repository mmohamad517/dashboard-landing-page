# Income roadmap

Written 2026-08-26. The plan for turning the site into income, in the order
the arithmetic demands.

## The equation everything follows from

```
income = visitors × conversion rate × price × times they buy
```

Current state: `0 visitors × ? × $14 × 1 = $0`

Improving the last three terms multiplies by zero. That is why the ordering
below is not an opinion — it is the only order that can work.

## Where the site stands

- Live since late August 2026. 49 pages: 40 guide articles, a pillar
  homepage, a product page, a free printable camping checklist page, and the
  legal and support pages.
- Verified in Google Search Console; sitemap submitted and fetched.
- One keyword had appeared at position ~92. No meaningful traffic yet.
- The product is one combined kit at $14, sold and delivered by Whop.
- No email list. No affiliate income. No advertising budget. Running cost $0.

**One honest correction carried forward from the research:** the site was
originally built around an outdoor-clothing keyword cluster. The owner's
Semrush export later showed that cluster is roughly 480 monthly searches,
while the camping-checklist cluster it sits next to is roughly 506,550.
`camping checklist printable` alone is about 74,000/month at KD 38 with no
AI Overview taking the clicks. That is why the checklist page exists and why
phase 1 builds outward from it rather than from the clothing articles.

---

## Phase 1 — Traffic (the only phase that does anything right now)

**Risk: 🟢 green.** Additive only. No existing URL, title or structure changes.

The asset most people never build is already here: 40 articles, none under
900 words, plus a page targeting a very high-volume, low-AI-Overview
keyword.

**Work:**
1. Build out the camping-checklist cluster — this is where the search volume
   actually is.
2. Add specific checklist pages that capture distinct searches: family
   camping, winter camping, one-night trips, first-time campers.
3. Refresh existing articles periodically rather than leaving them static.

**The honest timeline:** Google typically takes three to six months to give a
new site meaningful position. This phase is slow, boring, and produces no
money. There is no way around it, and trying to shortcut it with structural
changes is what breaks sites.

**Exit signal:** hundreds of impressions in Search Console rather than
single digits, and the first real clicks arriving.

---

## Phase 2 — Capture visitors before they leave

**Risk: 🟡 yellow.** Adds a third-party script, which costs page speed.
Requires the owner's explicit yes.

The biggest current leak: the free checklist downloads with nothing asked in
return. Every visitor who takes it is gone permanently and anonymously.

Trading the download for an email address turns a one-time visitor into
someone who can be sold to repeatedly. **This phase is what makes phase 4
possible**, which is the phase where the money actually is.

**Constraints to respect:**
- The site is static, so form handling requires an external service.
- That service's script costs page-speed, and mobile PageSpeed is currently
  93 after real work to get it there.
- Free-tier limits must be checked at implementation time, not assumed.

---

## Phase 3 — Same visitors, more sales

**Risk: 🟢 green.** Product-page copy and layout only. No URL changes.

Once Search Console shows which pages bring visitors and where they stop:

- Restructure the product page (`cro` skill).
- Strengthen the copy (`copywriting` skill).
- Add a clear refund guarantee to reduce purchase hesitation.

**Do not start this before there is data.** Without it, every change is a
guess, and guesses cannot be evaluated.

---

## Phase 4 — The second and third product

**Risk: 🟢 green.** New product files, new product page. Nothing existing moves.

This is the largest opportunity in the plan and the one most people miss.

| Scenario | Result |
|---|---|
| 100 buyers × one $14 product | $1,400 |
| 100 buyers × three $14 products | $4,200 |

Same traffic. Same marketing effort. No additional acquisition cost.

Someone who has already bought once is the easiest customer there is.
Candidates for product two and three: a printable checklist bundle, a trip
planning guide, a family edition of the deck.

This phase depends entirely on phase 2 — without an email list there is no
way to reach existing buyers.

---

## Phase 5 — Affiliates

**Risk: 🟡 yellow.** External links plus a required FTC disclosure.

Deliberately deferred. FlexOffers declined the site (Account #1558054) when
it was two days old with no traffic, which is the normal outcome; their
letter invites reapplication.

**Order when there is real traffic:**
1. **Payoneer first** — never attempted, and it addresses getting paid at all.
2. Reapply to FlexOffers after roughly two months of traffic.
3. **Avoid Amazon Associates for now.** Its three-sales-in-180-days rule
   closes the account, which burns the opportunity rather than delaying it.

---

## What we deliberately do not do

**Risk: 🔴 red.** Each of these can damage the site or the business.

| Rejected | Why |
|---|---|
| Physical dropshipping (Temu/Shein) | Breaks the owner's passive-income requirement; power banks are a regulated lithium-battery category; Temu-branded packaging defeats white-labelling |
| Mass-generated pages | Thin-content penalties apply site-wide, not per page |
| Invented reviews, ratings or statistics | Google manual action plus FTC exposure |
| Restructuring URLs | GitHub Pages serves no redirects — old URLs simply 404 |
| Paid advertising | No budget, and no data yet to know what would work |
| A reviews widget | Nothing to display, and an empty one signals no customers |

---

## The honest summary

Build content patiently for three to six months, capture emails, then
release a second product. Phase 4 is what turns the site from something that
sells occasionally into something that earns.

**And the part that has to be said:** this may not work. Plenty of sites
follow a sound plan and never make sales. This is the best plan available at
this budget with this asset, and its worst case costs nothing but time.

## What the owner does next

Open Search Console → Performance → 28 days in two to four weeks and send a
screenshot. Real data decides which phase we are actually in.
